Major Strands of Sociology — UPSC Comparison

A thinker-linked reference for building analytical, multi-perspective UPSC answers

1. Strand-by-Strand Comparison

Wright Mills → power/conflict; Garfinkel → actors construct social order
Conflict Perspective / Marxism Society is characterised by inequality, domination and conflict over resources and power Karl Marx, Engels Class struggle, mode of production, alienation, exploitation, ideology Economic determinism; underestimates culture, status and individual agency Weber → class + status + party; Durkheim → consensus/integration; Interactionists → micro-level meaning
Weberian / Interpretative Sociology Sociology should understand subjective meanings attached to social action Max Weber Verstehen, social action, ideal type, rationalisation, authority Difficult to establish objective/general laws; can underplay structures Marx → material/economic structures; Durkheim/Positivists → objective social facts
Phenomenology Social reality is constructed through people's everyday experiences and consciousness Edmund Husserl, Alfred Schutz Lifeworld, intersubjectivity, stock of knowledge, typification Highly subjective; difficult to generalise; neglects wider structures/power Marx → structural inequality; Durkheim → external social facts; Giddens → structure-agency connection
Symbolic Interactionism Society emerges through interaction and meanings attached to symbols Mead, Cooley, Herbert Blumer, Goffman Self, looking-glass self, significant/generalised others, definition of situation, labelling Micro-level focus; neglects large-scale structures and power Marx → class/power; Parsons → macro institutions; Feminists → gendered power structures
Ethnomethodology Studies how ordinary people produce and maintain social order in everyday life Harold Garfinkel Breaching experiments, indexicality, reflexivity, practical reasoning Excessively micro; ignores historical/institutional structures Marx/Weber → macro power/authority; Giddens → structuration
Exchange Theory Social relationships involve cost-benefit calculations and reciprocal exchange George Homans, Peter Blau Rewards, costs, reciprocity, exchange Reduces complex social relations to rational calculation Durkheim → moral solidarity; Mauss → gift and obligation; Marx → exploitation
Feminist Perspective Social institutions and knowledge are shaped by gender inequality and patriarchy Simone de Beauvoir, Ann Oakley, Sylvia Walby, Judith Butler Patriarchy, gender socialisation, public/private divide, intersectionality Some versions criticised for treating women as a homogeneous category Crenshaw → intersectionality; Butler → challenges fixed gender categories
Postmodernism Rejects one universal explanation; society is fragmented, plural and socially constructed Lyotard, Baudrillard, Foucault Grand narratives, simulacra, discourse, power/knowledge Relativism; difficulty explaining material inequality Marxists → material structures remain important; Habermas → defence of reason/universal communication
Critical Theory / Neo-Marxism Goes beyond economics to examine culture, ideology, media and domination Horkheimer, Adorno, Marcuse, Habermas Culture industry, ideology, communicative action Can be overly pessimistic about mass culture and agency Weber → multidimensional domination; Giddens → agency; Interactionists → active meaning-making
Structuration Theory Structure and agency are mutually constitutive Anthony Giddens Duality of structure, agency, structuration Abstract and difficult to operationalise empirically Marx → structural constraints; Interactionists → micro agency; Bourdieu → habitus/field
Bourdieu's Practice Theory Social practices emerge from interaction of habitus, capital and field Pierre Bourdieu Economic, cultural, social & symbolic capital; habitus; field; symbolic violence Concepts can be difficult to measure; may understate conscious agency Giddens → greater emphasis on agency/reflexivity; Marx → stronger economic/class emphasis
Rational Choice Theory Individuals are strategic actors who choose actions based on preferences, incentives and constraints James Coleman, George Homans Rational actor, utility, incentives Underplays norms, emotions, culture and structural constraints Durkheim → norms; Weber → value-oriented action; Bourdieu → habitus

3. The 4-Angle Framework for UPSC Answers

1. POSITIVIST — "What objective social forces cause this?"
→ Comte, Durkheim
2. INTERPRETATIVE / PHENOMENOLOGICAL — "What does this situation mean to the people experiencing it?"
→ Weber, Schutz, Mead, Blumer
3. CONFLICT — "Who has power, who benefits and who is disadvantaged?"
→ Marx, Dahrendorf, C. Wright Mills
4. FUNCTIONALIST — "What function does this institution/practice perform for the social system?"
→ Durkheim, Parsons, Merton

4. UPSC Analytical Formula

Structure → Meaning → Function → Power/Conflict

Example: Religion

  • Durkheim: Religion creates social solidarity.
  • Marx: Religion can legitimise domination.
  • Weber: Religious ideas can shape economic action.
  • Schutz: Examine the lived meaning of religion for believers.
  • Interactionism: Religious identity is produced through everyday interaction.

Result

Instead of writing only:

"According to Marx, religion is an instrument of domination."

Write:

"While Marx highlights the role of religion in legitimising class domination, Weber provides a contrasting interpretation by demonstrating how religious ideas can themselves influence economic behaviour, as illustrated by the Protestant ethic."

This creates thinker-to-thinker linkage, which makes the answer analytical rather than merely descriptive.
